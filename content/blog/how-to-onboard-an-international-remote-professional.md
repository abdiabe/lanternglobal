You found a strong candidate, agreed on terms, and set a start date. The next 30 days will do more to determine whether the relationship succeeds than anything that happened during hiring.

International remote onboarding has a few extra wrinkles: different time zones, no chance to learn by overhearing, and sometimes different workplace norms. A structured plan handles all of them.

## Before day one

Preparation prevents the most common first-week problem: a new team member waiting around for access.

**Access and accounts**

- Create individual accounts in every system they need, with only the permissions their role requires
- Enable multi-factor authentication
- Add them to the right channels, folders, and calendars
- Test logins before the start date if possible

**Documentation**

- A one-page role summary with the outcomes you expect in 90 days
- Step-by-step notes or short screen recordings for their first recurring tasks
- Examples of good work: a well-written client email, a clean report, a properly closed ticket
- A list of people they will work with and what each person owns

**Logistics**

- Confirm working hours and the daily overlap window in writing
- Share a holiday calendar that covers both countries
- Name a primary point of contact who will answer questions quickly in the first weeks

## Week 1: Orientation and first tasks

Spend the first week building context, not throughput.

- **Day 1:** a live welcome call, introductions to the team, a walkthrough of tools, and a review of the 90-day outcomes.
- **Days 2 and 3:** shadow the work. Walk through real examples together, ideally on screen share.
- **Days 4 and 5:** hand over two or three low-risk recurring tasks with a quick review of each result.

End the week with a 20-minute conversation: what is clear, what is confusing, and what would help.

## Week 2: Building ownership

Add responsibilities in a deliberate order, from lower risk to higher risk. Review work daily at first, then every other day. Give specific feedback:

- Instead of "this needs work," say "this summary is too long; aim for five bullet points, with decisions first."
- Instead of "be more proactive," say "when a vendor hasn't replied in two days, follow up and let me know."

Specific feedback is especially important across cultures, where indirect criticism can be easy to miss.

## Weeks 3 and 4: Toward independence

By now your professional should own most of the tasks in their first-month plan. Shift from reviewing everything to spot-checking. Move check-ins from daily to two or three times a week.

This is also a good time to ask them to improve the documentation. People new to a process often see gaps that experienced team members no longer notice.

## Communication norms that make remote work easier

Agree on a few simple norms early:

- **Response expectations:** for example, chat messages answered within an hour during overlap hours, email within one business day.
- **Status updates:** a short end-of-day note listing what was done, what is blocked, and what is next works well across time zones.
- **Asking questions:** make it explicit that questions are welcome. Some professionals hesitate to ask because they worry it signals weakness. Say clearly that you would rather answer a question than fix a mistake.
- **Escalation:** what counts as urgent, and how to reach someone when it happens.

## Bridging time zones

The Philippines is 12 or 13 hours ahead of U.S. Eastern Time, depending on daylight saving time. Whether your professional works U.S. hours or keeps a partial overlap:

- Protect the overlap window for meetings and questions
- Use the end-of-day note so the next shift starts with full context
- Record walkthroughs so learning does not depend on live meetings
- Be mindful of meeting times that fall late in their evening if they are not working U.S. hours

## The 30, 60, and 90-day reviews

Hold short, structured conversations at each milestone:

| Review | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 30 days | Are the basics working? Access, communication, first tasks |
| 60 days | Quality and consistency; which responsibilities to add next |
| 90 days | Progress against original outcomes; goals for the next quarter |

Use the outcomes you wrote before day one as the measuring stick, and invite feedback about what would help them do better work.

## Common onboarding mistakes

- **Starting with the hardest work.** Early wins build confidence on both sides.
- **No single point of contact.** Questions go unanswered and small issues grow.
- **Silence as feedback.** No news is not good news in remote work. Say what is working, too.
- **Assuming context.** Explain why tasks matter, not only how to do them.
- **Treating the professional as an outsider.** Include them in relevant team meetings and updates. People do their best work when they feel part of the team.
